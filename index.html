Her life

Biography

In Loving Memory of Mrs Regina Efiba Insaidoo (née Andoh)

Mrs Regina Efiba Insaidoo was born on 11 May 1956 to the late Mr B. B. Andoh and Mrs Elizabeth Andoh. She was the youngest of nine children on her mother’s side. As the beloved last-born, she was cherished and fiercely protected by her parents and older brothers and sisters. Her early years were filled with love, laughter and adventure.

Regina began her schooling in Sekondi and completed her secondary education at Nsein Senior Secondary School in Axim. It was there that she met her future husband, the late Mr J. K. Insaidoo, who was serving as a tutor at the school. Their friendship continued after she left Nsein, and in time it grew into a loving partnership.

She went on to study at Takoradi Polytechnic and the Ghana School of Languages, where she studied French. She became fluent in the language. After completing her studies, Regina married her sweetheart, John. In 1977, they moved to Lagos, Nigeria. Regina worked as a French tutor, while John taught mathematics at the University of Lagos.

In 1985, the couple returned to Ghana during the mass movement of Ghanaians from Nigeria, popularly known as “Ghana Must Go”. Regina was a hardworking woman who never liked to sit idle. She found work at the Great Western Hotel in Takoradi, formerly known as the Atlantic Hotel. She also ran small buying-and-selling ventures to support the family, while John taught at Fijai Secondary School.

In 1988, John took up a position with Southern Cross Mining Company in Konongo, and the couple moved there. Regina worked as a personal assistant to the managing director. Her colleagues admired her hard work, commitment, excellent typing skills and lovely nature. They also noticed her beauty, but it was her kindness and dedication that made her such a valued member of the team.

In 1990, Regina and John moved to Accra. She worked for several years as a secretary and office manager at Aikins Electrical. Managers, clients and colleagues came to appreciate her strong work ethic, humility and ability to bring people together. She had a special gift for keeping the peace.

Regina and John shared a deep belief in the value of education. In 2005, they founded Royal Crown International School near their home in Awoshie. Regina left her job at Aikins Electrical to help establish the school. John planned to retire and join her in managing it, but he died suddenly in August 2006. His passing left Regina heartbroken.

Even in her grief, Regina carried on with courage. She began running the school with just one classroom. She studied management at GIMPA and took several short courses in education to strengthen her skills. Through determination, hard work and careful management, she grew the school into one of the largest in the community.

The road was not always easy, but Regina remained faithful to her vision. Her journey inspired the school’s motto: “Perseverance Conquers All.” She was a persistent woman and an excellent negotiator, but she brought compassion as well as resolve to her work. Staff members looked up to her as a caring mother figure. She offered guidance and encouragement, helping staff and relatives find careers and opportunities both within the school and beyond it. She continued to lead Royal Crown International School until her untimely death after a short illness on 5 October 2026.

Regina and John were blessed with three children: Angela, Nana Faa and Paa Kwesi. She was also a proud grandmother to six grandchildren. Her family was at the centre of her life, and she gave them a home filled with love, warmth and laughter.

Regina had a natural way of connecting with people. She was warm, approachable and could strike up a conversation with almost anyone. If someone had a problem, she often knew a person who could help—and she was never shy about bringing people together. Her many friendships were a testament to the care she showed to others.

A committed Christian, Regina was a valued member of Victory Bible Church and worshipped at Dominion Sanctuary in Awoshie. She served in the Sanctuary Choir, Hymn Choir, Evangelism Team and Prestige Ladies. Her warm, bubbly and fun-loving personality endeared her to many. To her fellow choristers, she was not only a friend, but a much-loved mother figure.

Regina was also a member of the Full Gospel Businessmen’s Fellowship International, the East Legon Executive Keep Fit Club and the Achimota Keep Fit Club. She served in different capacities and took an active part in her church, fellowship and community.

In her younger years, Regina was a keen athlete who competed in long jump and long-distance running. She stayed active well into her later years. She enjoyed exercise and healthy eating, and encouraged those around her to look after their health too.

Her family and friends will remember the warmth, laughter and comfort she brought into their lives. Her family called her Sister Eba. Her nieces and nephews knew her as Aunty Eba and Eve, and her grandchildren called her Grandma Reggie. She was a loving mother, a wonderful aunt, a devoted sister and a faithful follower of Christ.

Regina lived a full 70 years, leaving behind a legacy of love, faithful service and perseverance. We give thanks for the many lives she touched and for the special place she will always hold in our hearts. We will miss her dearly, but we will carry her love and example with us.

Regina, da yie. May your soul rest in perfect peace.

Tribute from the In-laws

A tribute from her in-laws

Our dearest Mum,

Although we joined your family through marriage, you welcomed us with open hearts and made us feel that we truly belonged. You accepted us, loved us and treated us as your own. We will always thank God for the kindness and warmth you showed us.

Your love for God was at the heart of who you were. Your faith guided your life, and you encouraged us through your words, your prayers and the example you set. We take comfort in believing that you are now resting in the presence of the Lord you loved and served.

You also taught us to care for the bodies God has given us. You loved keeping fit and eating healthily, and you were always encouraging us to do the same. Sometimes, you even reminded us when we needed a little more exercise or a healthier choice! We will miss those loving reminders, along with your laughter and the care behind them.

You were kind, but you were also honest. When we got things wrong, you did not hesitate to correct us, but you did so with love. You wanted the best for us, and your guidance came from a place of deep care. We will remember your advice and try to live by the lessons you taught us.

We are deeply saddened that you are no longer with us. We will miss your conversations, your warm welcome and the comfort of knowing you were there. Yet we hold on to the hope we share in Christ: that death is not the end, and that one day we will meet again.

Thank you for welcoming us into your family, for loving us as your own and for trusting us with the people you cherished most. We will honour your memory by caring for the family you loved and by sharing the same kindness, faith and love you gave to us.

Rest peacefully in the Lord’s loving care, dear Mum. We thank God for the blessing of having you in our lives. You will always have a special place in our hearts.

With love, gratitude and prayers,
Your sons- and daughters-in-law

Tribute from the Nieces and Nephews

A tribute from her nieces

Let not your heart be troubled; you believe in God, believe also in Me. In My Father's house are many mansions; if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and receive you to Myself; that where I am, there you may be also.

John 14:1–3 (NKJV)

It is with a heavy heart and deep sadness that we write these words in memory of our beloved Aunty Regina.

It is difficult to accept that someone who brought so much happiness, laughter and warmth into our lives is no longer with us. Yet, as we mourn her passing, we also celebrate the beautiful life she lived and the wonderful memories she leaves behind.

Aunty Regina was truly one of a kind. She had a bubbly personality, a wonderful sense of humour and a special way of making people smile. She was one of those people whose presence could light up a room. Her laughter was infectious, her spirit was lively, and she had a way of making even ordinary moments feel special. You could always expect a little humour, a funny comment or a moment of laughter whenever she was around. That was the beauty of Aunty Regina. She brought joy simply by being herself. Her personality made her memorable, and her warmth made her special. These are the qualities that will remain with us long after the sadness of her passing.

Although distance may have separated us, the bond of family and the love we shared remained.

Anyone who knew Aunt Regi knew how much she loved staying active. Whether she was out on a long walk or heading to a keep-fit class, she embraced each day with energy, strength, and joy.

When we visited her in Ghana last year, she was always encouraging us to join her early morning fitness classes. She would be ready and eager to go, urging us to get up and come along. We would smile and tell her, “Don’t worry, we’ll follow you!” But at 6:00 a.m., in the Ghanaian heat, there was absolutely no chance of us keeping up with her enthusiasm and determination.

That was Aunt Regi, full of life, discipline, and a spirit that inspired everyone around her.

Her faith was the anchor of her life, guiding her kindness and the way she cared for everyone around her. While our hearts are heavy, there is deep comfort in knowing that she is now at peace in the presence of her Savior. We picture her reunited in heaven with her beloved mother, father, and the siblings who went before her, walking together in joy once again. We will miss her energy, her warmth, and her faithful spirit every single day.

Today, our thoughts and prayers are also with our family in Ghana, as we come together in grief to say our final goodbyes. Aunty Regi, you have left us with memories that we will treasure forever. We will miss your laughter, your bubbly nature, your humour and the happiness you brought into our lives. There will be moments when something reminds us of you, and we will smile through our tears, remembering the wonderful person you were.

Life may be temporary, but the impact of a beautiful soul is everlasting. Your journey on earth has ended, but your story will continue through the memories you created and the lives you touched.

Aunty Regi, you came, you lived, you laughed, and you left beautiful memories. You will be dearly missed but never forgotten.

May your gentle soul rest in perfect peace.

Tribute from the Classical Choir

In Loving Memory of Auntie Regina Insaidoo

“Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of His faithful servants.”

Psalm 116:15

Today, the Classical Choir joins countless hearts in mourning the passing of our beloved Auntie Regina Insaidoo.

Auntie Regina was a woman whose presence brought warmth, love, kindness and encouragement especially with her presence at rehearsals.
She touched lives in ways that words cannot fully express, and her memory will forever remain a cherished part of our lives as a choir.

We are grateful for the moments we shared with her—the smiles, the gentle words, the fellowship and the love she showed to all.
She is such a disciplined person who does not toy with her time and fitness.
She will walk from home to rehearsal and back. Therefore, when it is her time to leave, she will graciously leave without hesitation nor apology.
Her life was a blessing, and her legacy will continue to speak even though she is no longer with us physically.

We mourn her departure, but we do not mourn without hope. We take comfort in the assurance that she has gone to rest in the loving arms of her Maker.

Auntie Regina, your voice may be silent, but the beautiful memories you left behind will continue to sing in our hearts. Your kindness will be remembered, your love will be cherished, and your legacy will live on.

From the Classical Choir, we say:
Rest peacefully, dear Auntie Regina.
Your labour is ended, your journey is complete, no more sickness, no more pain, no more walking to and from rehearsals.
You fought your fight.
Your crown awaits.
Until we meet again dear gallant soldier, may your gentle soul rest in perfect peace.

The Classical Choir
